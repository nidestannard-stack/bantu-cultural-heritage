About Our Digital Museum

The Bantu & Hawaiian Cultural Heritage Museum is an educational digital museum project developed by an anthropology student at the University of Hawaiʻi at Hilo.

The museum explores the cultural knowledge, craftsmanship, traditions, and innovations of Bantu-speaking African communities and Native Hawaiian society.

Our Mission

Our mission is to document and share cultural heritage through historical research, original photography, and the study of physical objects.

By comparing cultural practices and material objects, the museum encourages visitors to appreciate the distinctive knowledge systems developed by different communities.

Why Compare These Cultures?

Although Bantu-speaking African communities and Native Hawaiian society have different histories, both offer important examples of environmental knowledge, craftsmanship, and cultural expression.

Our comparisons are not intended to suggest that these cultures are identical or that one is superior to another. Instead, they highlight how people develop knowledge and traditions within their own environments and histories.

Our Collections

The museum features exhibits on calabashes, ankle ornaments, water and agriculture, and engineering and innovation. Future collections may explore additional traditional technologies and everyday objects.

The long-term vision is to develop a collection of physical artifacts supported by original photographs, documentation, and field research.

Educational Purpose

This website was created as part of a CS-200 Web Technology course project at the University of Hawaiʻi at Hilo.

It is an evolving student research project. Object identifications, cultural interpretations, and historical information will be expanded as additional research becomes available.
