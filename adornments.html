Exhibit 2: Ankle Ornaments Across Cultures

Across Hawaiʻi and southern Africa, natural materials have been used to create ornaments associated with movement and cultural expression. This exhibit compares a Hawaiian ankle ornament with Shangaan maronga.

Hawaiian Ankle Ornament

Object and photograph: Nide Stannard, personal collection.

This photographed ornament features brown beads, pale natural-looking components, colored beads, and cord. Hawaiian ankle adornments, including some forms of kupeʻe, have been associated with hula and cultural expression.

The specific maker, age, and traditional function of this photographed object have not yet been verified.

Southern African Leg Rattles and Shangaan Maronga

Comparative photograph: leg rattles, Lake Malawi Museum, Malawi. Photograph by TimCowley (2014), CC BY-SA 4.0. Image source: Wikimedia Commons. These are not identified as Shangaan maronga.

The photograph shows leg rattles from the Lake Malawi Museum, not a documented example of Shangaan maronga. It illustrates a related tradition of wearable sound-making objects in southern Africa. Maronga are ankle rattles documented among Shangaan communities in Limpopo, South Africa.

The Phansi Museum reference below documents Shangaan maronga separately. Although both examples involve wearable rattles, their cultural contexts should not be conflated.

Research reference: Phansi Museum: Maronga Ankle Rattles

Comparative Interpretation

Both traditions demonstrate how natural materials can be transformed into wearable objects. Differences in construction, historical context, and cultural use are important. Similar appearances do not establish shared origins or identical meanings.
